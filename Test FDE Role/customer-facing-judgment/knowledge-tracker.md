# Question Log

## Q001
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Concept:** CJ9 — Discovery-only customer-conversation simulation (Anthropic)
**Difficulty:** Level 3 (Application/Scenario)
**Question:** Live ~30min simulated discovery call. Assistant played Head of Claims Operations at a mid-size regional insurance company exploring Claude for claims processing. Candidate ran discovery, no pitching, no demo.

### My Answer
> First pass: excellent workflow/technical discovery — mapped the FNOL->adjuster->SIU->customer service->legal pipeline, surfaced channel fragmentation (phone/app/email/one-way SMS), quantified pain points (15-day cycle-time target vs. 22-day actual, 24hr vs. 36-48hr FNOL-to-adjuster handoff target vs. actual), claim-type segmentation with different targets (auto/property/liability/total-loss), the third-party dependency gap (no SLA leverage over contractors/repair shops/medical providers, manual chasing, no overdue-flagging system), and current architecture (legacy 12-year-old on-prem CMS in maintenance mode, siloed call-center/app/email/document systems, no near-term replacement planned). Then asked to transition to pitching Claude's benefits.
>
> After being told discovery wasn't complete (missing the Administrative & Political bucket entirely), asked a second round covering: "why now" (CJ8's exact framing question — surfaced a regulatory near-miss, sliding CSAT scores/viral complaints, and competitive pressure from a rival's AI-driven cycle-time press release), timeline (soft, board-meeting-driven, ~10-12 weeks, not a technical deadline), Champion (self-identified as the buyer, weakly — a "figure it out" mandate, not real executive sponsorship), Blocker (two sources: IT's security/vendor-review anxiety, and adjusters fearing the tool could grade their performance or threaten headcount), and Success Metric (contested — compliance wants regulatory-risk reduction, CFO wants cost/headcount efficiency, CEO wants competitive optics, no agreed single metric).

### Assessment
🟡 Mostly Correct (first pass) → upgraded after 2nd pass closed the gap

### What I Got Right
- Technical/workflow discovery was excellent from the start: layered questions, quantified findings, no jumping to solution, no pitching, no demo — matches every explicit pass signal for this round.
- The third-party-dependency finding (no SLA leverage, manual chasing) was a genuinely sharp, specific discovery, not a generic question.
- Once prompted, closed the Administrative & Political bucket fully and precisely: weak/self-identified Champion, two distinct Blockers (one technical/IT, one human/trust — correctly distinct from each other), and a contested Success Metric across three stakeholders. All four (Champion/Blocker/Success Metric/timeline) landed in one clean round of questions.
- Correctly asked CJ8's "why now" framing question, which surfaced real, usable business-driver context (regulatory near-miss, CSAT decline, competitive pressure).

### What I Missed
- Ended the discovery phase prematurely on the first pass — asked to transition into pitching before touching the political/administrative side at all, despite having covered the technical side thoroughly. The round's real skill is knowing discovery isn't done just because the technical/workflow half feels complete.
- Minor delivery issue in the second-pass message: mixed in reassurance/conclusions ("I don't think IT has anything to worry about," "our solutions team can reach something up") before/alongside the actual questions — a small version of stating an answer before asking, which is exactly the anti-pattern this round penalizes at a larger scale (opening with a demo/solution).

### Model Answer
> A complete discovery call covers both halves before considering pitching: the technical/workflow side (what was done excellently here) AND the Administrative & Political side (Champion, Blocker, Success Metric — CJ1's Discovery Checklist) plus the "why now" framing (CJ8). Only once both are covered — and ideally the Success Metric is at least surfaced as contested/undefined if not resolved — does it make sense to ask about transitioning toward a proposed approach, and even then, staying discovery-only for the full round (per CJ9) is safest until explicitly cued otherwise.

### Knowledge Gap
Strong instinct for technical discovery depth; weaker instinct for treating the political/organizational side as equally mandatory before considering discovery "done." Also: a tendency to slip in reassuring statements/conclusions rather than pure questions once talking about stakeholders' concerns (IT, budget) — worth deliberately avoiding "I don't think X needs to worry" framing and asking instead ("what's X's specific concern here?").

### Score (1-10, per ../scoreboard.md rubric)
9 — first-pass technical discovery was a 9-10 by itself; docked to an overall 9 for the premature pitch-transition attempt (corrected immediately once flagged) and the minor reassurance-before-asking pattern in the second pass.

### Memory Priority
High — the "technical discovery complete != discovery complete" distinction is a real, general FDE interview risk (this round's ~60% filter rate at Anthropic suggests many candidates make exactly this mistake).

### Follow-up Required
No — clean recovery once flagged, both halves now demonstrated. Light retest later with a different customer scenario to confirm the political-bucket habit holds without a nudge next time.
